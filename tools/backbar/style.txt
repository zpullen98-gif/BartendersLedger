The liquor shelf: how a bottle is written (4 November 2026)

WHO READS IT
The owner works the floor at Brennan's, New Orleans, and studies to master bartender. The page sits
under the Brennan's tab of the Bartender's Ledger, in Bar studies after Coffee and tea. It is every
bottle the owner photographed on the back bar on 4 November 2026 (tools/backbar/photos/), each with a
deep dive and the words to present it at the table. A reader opens a bottle because a guest asked, or
to learn the shelf before a shift. Write as a master bartender talks to a trained colleague: plain,
warm, exact, concrete.

ONE BOTTLE = ONE FRAGMENT
tools/backbar/fragments/<id>.json, the id from tools/backbar/plan.json. tools/backbar/schema.mjs is
the one validator; build-backbar.mjs refuses a fragment it rejects. Fields, all required:
  id          the plan's id
  name        the plan's name, the bottle as its label prints it (at most 60 characters)
  respell     how to say it, capitals for the stressed syllable, hyphens between syllables
              (BEN-eh-dik-TEEN); "" for a name an English speaker reads as written (at most 60)
  aka         other names a guest or a menu uses, a list, may be empty ("Chartreuse Verte")
  group       the plan's group
  producer    the company that makes it, as its own site names it (on companies.json; at most 60
              characters). The maker, never the brand's current owner or parent group
  place       where it is made: the distillery's town and region (at most 12 words)
  photo       the plan's photograph number
  confidence  the plan's (clear or probable). A probable bottle carries at least one ask
  facts       five short strings, each a plain statement:
                base   what it is distilled or made from (at most 20 words)
                age    the age statement or how long it rests; "No age statement" where none (16)
                cask   what it rests in, or "Not aged in wood" (20)
                abv    as the label states it, "40% ABV (80 proof)"; for a yearly or batch release
                       (the plan's yearly: true) it begins "varies by release" and may say how the
                       label gives it ("varies by release; the label states each year's proof") (14)
                law    the legal category and the rule it is made under (25)
  made        how it is made: the base, the still, the botanicals or the secret as the house
              admits it, the wood, the time (40 to 90 words)
  taste       how it tastes and feels, in concrete words: nose, palate, finish (20 to 60 words)
  lines       the tableside lines, each said aloud to a guest:
                s10   the ten second line: names the bottle and says what it is (12 to 25 words)
                s20   the twenty second line (25 to 50 words)
                s45   the forty-five second line, the five-star tableside presentation: open on
                      the bottle as the label prints it, then where and how it is made, how it
                      tastes, how we pour it, and one line of story (90 to 110 words)
                sale  exactly two sentences: the hook, then the pour or the drink (15 to 45 words)
              s10 and s45 name the bottle (its name or an aka).
  serve       how we pour it, each at most 20 words, none empty:
                neat     the neat pour and its temperature
                rocks    on ice, or why not
                glass    the glass
                classic  the classic cocktail it belongs in, by name
  guest       one or two sentences a server says when a guest asks what it is (12 to 40 words)
  story       where it comes from, historic founders only (30 to 110 words)
  watch       2 to 4 lines, each at most 25 words: what guests ask and what you say, the mix-ups
              (Yellow for Green, a reposado for a blanco), what goes wrong in the pour
  asks        the lineup questions this bottle needs answered, each at most 30 words: what the
              photographs and research cannot settle (which batch is open, which expression).
              A list, may be empty for a clear bottle; a probable bottle keeps the plan's ask
              until research settles it
  house       copied from the plan: { match: [...], family: [...] } (see THE HOUSE)
  library     copied from the plan: { producer, cask, canon: [...] }
  recall      [{ id:'recall', prompt, answer }]: the prompt names the bottle and asks what it is,
              how it is made or how it is poured (at most 25 words); the answer 15 to 70 words.
              The card's key is 'Back Bar · <id>/recall' (keys.v1.json)
  quiz        exactly 3 questions [{ id:'q1'|'q2'|'q3', q, options:[4 strings], a:0..3, explain }]:
              one fact each, the stem at most 30 words and never containing the answer, four
              options of one kind (four places, four years, four grains), each at most 12 words,
              one right answer and three a trained person might pick, no "all of the above", no
              option much longer than the others, the three answers not all in one position,
              explain at most 45 words
  sources     at least two { title, url } with https urls the author read: the producer's own
              page first, then a second independent source; in-app sources may be added as
              { title, url:"" } (a Cask Atlas chapter, a Library producer)
Whole entry at most 750 reading words (made, taste, lines, serve, guest, story, watch, asks).

A CONFIRM ROW
A bottle no label on the photographs names. It lives in plan.json, never as a fragment, and carries
only: id, name (what can be read, or a short description), group "confirm", photo, where (where it
stands), read (what can be read on it), ask (the lineup question, at most 30 words). When an author
cannot confirm a probable bottle from the photograph and two sources, the author moves its row to
the confirm group in plan.json (name, where, read, ask; no chunk, house or library) and writes no
fragment for it.

THE HOUSE
Never state what Brennan's pours, stocks, charges or builds. House facts come only from the pack,
read live by the page: At the house lists the pack's drinks and dishes whose printed spec, printed
ingredients, kept ingredient names or linked ingredient components name one of the bottle's
house.match terms; From the same house lists the pack's producer profiles whose maker (the profile's
"who") names one of its house.family terms, with the items they belong to.
The matching rule (schema.mjs houseUses and houseFamily are the reference, the page and the gate
use the same rule): text and term are folded (accents off, lower case, every run of other
characters one space) and a term counts only as a whole phrase. An occurrence is dropped when it
lies inside a longer occurrence of another bottle's match term ("Stagg" inside "George T. Stagg").
So a match term is the product's exact name, specific enough that it can only mean THIS bottle:
"Green Chartreuse", never "Chartreuse"; "Don Julio Reposado", never "Don Julio". A probable bottle
whose expression is unknown has no match term until it is known. A comparison in the pack ("the
Last Word is gin, maraschino and lime") is never a use.
Never name the house in a fragment: the page names it from the pack. Write "we", "our" or "the
house" where a line needs it. Nothing about the house's stock, rotation, measures or prices.

FACTS
A figure, a date, a proof, an age or a cask needs the producer's own page or two independent
sources. A yearly or batch release (the Antique Collection, O.F.C., Stagg) says each release's proof
differs and the label gives it: never one year's figure as the bottle's. Where sources disagree, say
"about" or give the range; where nothing confirms it, leave it out, and turn a doubt about which
bottle it is into an ask. Read the photograph for what the label states. Nothing volatile: no
allocation, lottery, scarcity, release or shelf price, secondary market, current owner, parent
group, acquisition or portfolio; no current master distiller or blender. History is fine: the year a
distillery was built, renamed or rebuilt.

PEOPLE
Name nobody living, ever: no current distiller, blender, owner, family member, bartender or writer
("the master distiller", "the founding family"). Historic founders and historic figures the bottle
is named for may be named (George T. Stagg, Thomas H. Handy, Jean Martell). people.json lists
living people never to name; schema.mjs also refuses a run of capitalised words that proper-nouns.json,
companies.json and the app's own names do not allow, so a new place, product or historic name is
added to proper-nouns.json with the source that shows it, and a person who may be living never is.

LANGUAGE
British spelling (flavour, colour, ageing, centre, litre, savoury, practise as the verb, -ise).
No em dash, no en dash, no spaced hyphen or double hyphen used as a dash: use a comma, a colon or a
full stop. Ranges with "to": "12 to 15 years". Nothing at or above U+2190: no arrows, no degree
sign, no fraction glyphs, no emoji. Curly quotes and the accented letters of names (Bénédictine,
Añejo, Cortés, Patrón) are fine. No superlative and nothing that sells rather than teaches ("the
best", "finest", "smoothest", "rarest", "oldest", "legendary", "iconic", "sought-after", "perfect",
"unique", "world-class"). No price, no currency sign, no cost. No allergen or dietary talk at all
(the page carries the one fixed pointer), no health claim ("aids digestion", "medicinal benefits").
Proof and ABV as the label prints them: "45% ABV (90 proof)".
