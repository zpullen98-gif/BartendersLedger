The Coffee and tea page: how an entry is written (18 October 2026)

WHO READS IT
The owner works the floor at Brennan's, New Orleans, and studies to master bartender. The page sits
under the Brennan's tab of the Bartender's Ledger. Brennan's own coffees come first, drawn live from
the house pack; every other entry is the general craft a server or bartender needs when a guest asks.

ONE ENTRY = ONE FRAGMENT
tools/brew/fragments/<id>.json, the id from tools/brew/plan.json. Fields, all required unless marked:
  id          the plan's id
  name        the plan's name (at most 40 characters)
  respell     how to say it, capitals for the stressed syllable, hyphens between syllables
              (kor-TAH-doh); "" for a plain English name (at most 40 characters)
  aka         other names a guest uses, a list (may be empty)
  group, kind the plan's
  caffeine    one plain sentence on how much caffeine and from where (at most 25 words)
  house       copied from the plan, never invented: { printed:true, packIds } or
              { printed:false, askId, ask } with optional uses; when printed is false and the plan's
              ask is empty, write ask as one sentence (at most 30 words) that says it is not printed on
              the menus and what to ask at lineup
  what        what it is: the drink, where it comes from, what makes it itself (35 to 90 words)
  taste       how it tastes and feels in the mouth (15 to 60 words)
  make        how to make it, the general method:
                steps   3 to 8 numbered steps in order, each at most 30 words, imperative
                ratio   the proportion (at most 14 words), "" if none
                dose    what goes in, by weight or volume (at most 14 words)
                temp    the temperatures, "195 to 205F (90 to 96C)" style (at most 14 words), "" if none
                time    how long (at most 14 words), "" if none
                cup     the cup or glass and its size (at most 14 words)
  serve       how it goes to the table: the cup warmed or chilled, the saucer and spoon, what rides
              with it, when to bring it (25 to 80 words)
  story       where it comes from and what a guest likes to hear (30 to 110 words)
  guest       one or two sentences a server says to a guest (12 to 40 words)
  watch       2 to 4 things that go wrong or that a guest asks, each at most 30 words
  library     copied from the plan (the renderer resolves it)
  table       copied from the plan
  search      copied from the plan
  recall      [{ id:'make', prompt, answer }]: prompt at most 25 words (the drink by name, asking how it
              is made or what is in it), answer 15 to 70 words (the build, the cup, the taste)
  quiz        exactly 3 questions [{ id:'q1'|'q2'|'q3', q, options:[4 strings], a:0..3, explain }]:
              the stem at most 30 words and never containing the answer, each option at most 12 words,
              one correct answer, three wrong ones a trained person might pick, no "all of the above",
              no option much longer than the others, explain at most 45 words
  sources     [{ title, url }] https links the author read in a search result, plus the in-app
              sources as { title, url:"" } (for example the Library lesson or the World Table entry)
  video       filled by the video finder, not the author (see VIDEOS); the author writes null
Whole entry at most 600 words.

THE HOUSE
Never state how Brennan's makes anything. House facts come only from the pack: the printed names,
prices, sections and ingredients of the six house records, their components and their lineup
questions. Every method is "a general method, not the house build"; the page says so once on every
detail. When an entry is printed (chicory coffee, single origin, cold brew, Irish coffee, espresso
martini, coffee with liqueur and cream), say in "what" or "serve" how the house's version relates to
the general drink, only from the pack (for example the Café Glacé starts from the house's chicory
cold brew). Never name the house in the fragment: the renderer names it from the pack. Write "our"
or "the house" if you must.

FACTS
A fact rests on an in-app source (the Ledger's Library lessons and cards in js/data-coffee.js, the
Library cocktails and zero-proof drinks, the World Table's Cuisine entries "Chicory coffee" and
"Café brûlot", its recipes and lexicon, the Brennan's pack), or on two search results from different
sites. Where the Library gives a number (a ratio, a temperature, a time), use it or reconcile with it;
never contradict it silently. Where sources disagree, give the range. No measures invented for a
house drink. No health claims, no caffeine promises, no allergen verdicts ("dairy free", "safe for").
Never name a person, living or dead, and never a business but a product or brand a guest orders by
name (Tullamore Dew, Kahlua, Frangelico are fine; a café, a barista, a writer, an inventor are not):
"an Irish airport chef in the 1940s", not his name.

LANGUAGE
British spelling (flavour, colour, favourite, savoury, centre, litre, practise as the verb, -ise).
No em dash, no en dash, no spaced hyphen or double hyphen used as a dash: use a comma, a colon or a
full stop. Ranges with "to": "25 to 30 seconds". Temperatures Fahrenheit first with Celsius in
brackets. Nothing at or above U+2190: no arrows, no degree sign ("F" and "C" alone), no fractions
glyphs (write "a half"), no emoji, no "≈". Curly quotes and the accented letters of names (café,
brûlot, frappé) are fine. Plain, warm, exact; no superlatives ("the best", "perfect", "amazing",
"delicious", "unique"), no marketing.

VIDEOS (the video finder and the video refuter, not the author)
A video is an external link the owner taps; nothing is embedded. Order of preference:
1. a Library film already checked (js/data-coffee-films.js; the plan's "film" names the lesson): copy
   its id, title and channel exactly, verifiedBy "coffee-films";
2. a video the house pack already carries for this drink (house.videos): verifiedBy "pack";
3. a new one found by WebSearch: one search for the plan's search phrase on youtube.com, a second for
   the exact title found; it counts only when both searches return the same www.youtube.com/watch?v=
   id with the same exact title, and the channel is named in a result's title or summary. No Shorts,
   no playlists, no music videos. Record both queries and what each returned in
   tools/brew/videos/<id>.json. verifiedBy "websearch".
The fragment's video: { url, id, title, channel, channelKind:'company'|'person', label, watchFor,
verifiedBy, checkedOn:'2026-10-18' }. label is our own few words (at most 8: "How to steam milk for a
cappuccino"); watchFor what to watch for in it (at most 40 words). A channel that is a person's (a
barista, a writer, anyone named) is channelKind 'person': the shipped fragment keeps title and channel
empty and the real ones stay only in tools/brew/videos/<id>.json. A company channel (a roaster, a
tea house, a machine maker, a magazine) is 'company' and its name may show. When no video passes,
video is null and the page offers the search link alone.
