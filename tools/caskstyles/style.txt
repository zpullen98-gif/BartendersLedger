At the table, every style: how a whisky style is written (5 November 2026)

WHO READS IT
The owner works the floor at a New Orleans restaurant and studies to master bartender. The page sits
in the Bartender's Ledger's Whisky & Brandy tab (the Cask Atlas, key casks): "At the table: every
style", thirty-four whisky styles in five families, each opened at #/casks/style/<id>. A reader opens a
style because a guest named it ("something wheated", "an Islay", "a pot still"), or to learn the
styles before a shift. The Cask Atlas chapters teach the category at length; a style entry is the
working page: the rule in a sentence, what is in the glass, and the words to present it at the table.
Write as a master whisky educator talks to a trained colleague on the floor: plain, warm, exact,
concrete. Every line is sayable aloud to a guest.

ONE STYLE = ONE FRAGMENT
tools/caskstyles/fragments/<id>.json, the id from tools/caskstyles/plan.json. tools/caskstyles/schema.mjs
is the one validator; build-caskstyles.mjs refuses a fragment it rejects. Fields, all required, in
this order (word caps are reading words, split on spaces):
  id          the roster's id
  name        the roster's name, exactly
  respell     how to say the name, capitals for the stressed syllable, hyphens between syllables
              (EYE-luh, KAM-bul-town, mee-zoo-NAH-rah); "" for a name an English speaker reads as
              written (at most 60 characters)
  family      copied from the roster (american, scotch, irish, japanese, canadian)
  law         the rule that makes it this style, stated in our own words close to the rule as written,
              with the instrument named: 27 CFR 5.143 (the United States standards of identity) or
              27 CFR 5.88 (bottled in bond), the Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009, the Irish whiskey
              technical file, the Japan Spirits & Liqueurs Makers Association standard (an association
              standard, not a law, and the law field says so), Canada's Food and Drug Regulations, a
              state law by its name (Tennessee Code 57-2-106, 2013). A style with no legal definition
              (wheated, high rye, four grain, small batch, Kentucky-style rye, Monongahela, Mizunara)
              says so first ("No legal category of its own: ...") and then gives the rule of the
              category it sits in. A proposal is called a proposal, never a rule (25 to 90 words)
  made        how it is made: the grains or the malt, the mash, the stills, the cask, the time, what
              varies from house to house (40 to 90 words)
  taste       how it tastes and feels, in concrete words: nose, palate, finish, and the range across
              the style, never a promise for every bottle ("often", "usually", "in most") (20 to 60)
  lines       the tableside lines, each said aloud to a guest:
                s10   the ten second line: names the style and says what it is (12 to 25 words)
                s20   the twenty second line: the rule and the taste in brief (25 to 50 words)
                s45   the forty-five second line, the professional tableside presentation: what the
                      style is, where and by what rule, how it is made, how it tastes, how we pour it,
                      and one line of story (90 to 110 words)
                sale  exactly two sentences: the hook, then the pour or the drink (15 to 45 words)
              s10 and s45 name the style (its name or one of the roster's terms, as a whole phrase).
  serve       how we pour it: the ones that fit, in this order, at least three, each at most 20 words:
                neat      the neat pour and its temperature
                water     a few drops of water, and what they open
                rocks     on ice, or why not
                highball  the long drink with soda or ginger ale, where the style suits one
                glass     the glass (always given)
                classic   the classic it belongs in, by the canon drink's name (always given when the
                          roster gives canon drinks; left out when it gives none, as for Mizunara)
              No pour size, no measure stated as ours.
  guest       2 to 4 of what guests ask: { q, a }, q the guest's own question (at most 20 words,
              ending with a question mark), a what you say (at most 40 words)
  compare     how it differs from its neighbour style, the roster's neighbour, named by its name or a
              roster term: the rule first, then the glass (20 to 60 words)
  benchmarks  3 to 6 houses or brands to name as examples: distilleries and brands, historic or
              working, each on houses.json; nobody living, and never a person as such (a brand that
              carries a historic founder's name, Jack Daniel's or W. L. Weller, is a brand)
  shelf       copied from the roster: the liquor shelf's bottles of this style, each a door to its
              page (#/menu/backbar/<id>); may be empty
  chapters    copied from the roster: the Cask Atlas chapters, the first the one it belongs to
  canon       copied from the roster: the canon drinks (COCKTAILS slugs); may be empty
  recall      [{ id:'recall', prompt, answer }]: the prompt names the style and asks the rule, how it
              is made or how to present it (at most 25 words, a question); the answer 15 to 70 words.
              The card's key is 'Cask Styles · <id>/recall' (keys.v1.json)
  quiz        exactly 3 questions [{ id:'q1'|'q2'|'q3', q, options:[4 strings], a:0..3, explain }]:
              one fact each, the stem a question of at most 30 words never containing the answer,
              four options of one kind (four rules, four years, four grains, four places), each at
              most 12 words, one right answer and three a trained person might pick, no "all of the
              above", no option much longer than the others, the three answers not all in one
              position, the explain at most 45 words. One question on the rule is usual
  sources     at least two { title, url } with https urls the author read: the rule itself first
              (eCFR or Cornell's LII, legislation.gov.uk, the technical file, the association's own
              page), then the producers' and independent pages; in-app sources may be added as
              { title, url:"" } (a Cask Atlas chapter)
Whole entry at most 800 reading words (law, made, taste, lines, serve, guest, compare).

AT THE TABLE
The forty-five is what a server says when a guest asks "tell me about it": it opens on the style by
name, places it (the country, the region, the rule in a clause), says how it is made in one or two
concrete steps, gives three or four tastes a guest will find, says how we pour it, and ends on one
line of story (a date, a founding, a law). It never lectures and never sells: no "you'll love", no
verdict on quality. The sale is two sentences a server can say while the guest decides: a hook a
guest remembers, then the pour or the drink. The ten and twenty are the same presentation cut
down, not new material. A guest question that the page answers is phrased as a guest phrases it
("Is it sweeter?", "Where does the smoke come from?").

THE HOUSE
A style page never names the house and never says what the house pours, stocks, charges or builds.
"We" and "our" are the server's voice in how we pour it. The shelf links are study links to the
liquor shelf's pages, which carry their own rules; a style says nothing about a bottle's batch, year
or proof beyond what that bottle's page says. No allergen or dietary talk at all.

FACTS
A rule, a figure, a date or a founding needs the rule itself or two independent sources. Where
sources disagree, say "about" or give the range; where nothing confirms it, leave it out. The law
field states the rule as it stands on 5 November 2026, and says so when a change is proposed but
not made (the 2026 consultation on the Irish whiskey technical file is a proposal). Known
corrections the Cask Atlas review makes and every style keeps (PLAN.md B): the American single malt
standard was published on 18 December 2024 and took effect on 19 January 2025; the Japanese
standard is an association standard (2021, the transition ended 31 March 2024), and the
association's 2025 logo is the association's; the Tennessee law is the 2013 state law with its
exception; a mix of straight whiskies of one type from one state stays straight; wheat is often
found softer and rounder, the grain one influence among yeast, barrel, proof and age. A region is
evidence of geography, not of flavour: say what most of its houses do and name the exceptions.
Nothing volatile: no allocation, lottery, scarcity, release or shelf price, secondary market,
current owner, parent group, acquisition or portfolio, no current master distiller or blender, no
count of working distilleries stated as fixed ("about ten"). History is fine: the year a distillery
was founded, closed, renamed or rebuilt, the year a law was made.

PEOPLE
Name nobody living, ever: no current distiller, blender, owner, family member, bartender or writer
("the master distiller", "the founding family"). Historic founders and figures may be named
(Masataka Taketsuru, Shinjiro Torii, Aeneas Coffey, Pappy Van Winkle). tools/caskstyles/people.json
and tools/backbar/people.json list living people never to name; schema.mjs also refuses a run of
capitalised words that proper-nouns.json (this pipeline's and the shelf's), houses.json and the
app's own names do not allow, so a new place, house or historic name is added to proper-nouns.json
or houses.json with the source that shows it, and a person who may be living never is.

LANGUAGE
British spelling in our own words (flavour, colour, ageing, centre, litre, savoury, practise as the
verb, -ise); a legal name stays as the law prints it (bourbon whisky, Tennessee whiskey). Whisky for
Scotch, Japanese and Canadian, whiskey for Irish and American, as the producers write it. No em
dash, no en dash, no spaced hyphen or double hyphen used as a dash: use a comma, a colon or a full
stop. Ranges with "to": "12 to 15 years"; a statute number keeps its hyphens (57-2-106). Nothing at
or above U+2190: no arrows, no degree sign, no fraction glyphs, no emoji. Curly quotes and the
accented letters of names are fine. No superlative at all and nothing that sells rather than
teaches: not "the best", "finest", "smoothest", "oldest", "largest", "smokiest", "the most",
"legendary", "iconic", "cult", "premium", "perfect", "unique", "world-class" (schema.mjs holds the
list). No price, no currency sign, no cost. No health claim. Proof and ABV as a label prints them,
"45% ABV (90 proof)"; a rule's strength in ABV with the proof beside it where the American rule
gives one, "no more than 80% ABV (160 proof)".
